A. Preliminary Experiments Details
In this experiment, we selected four jailbreak methods(DeepInception (Li et al., 2023), ReNeLLM (Ding et al., 2024),
CodeAttack (Jha & Reddy, 2023), CodeChameleon (Lv et al., 2024)) and one over-refusal dataset(Or-Bench (Cui et al.,
2025) for evaluation. Both the assessment of jailbreak results and the determination of over-refusal followed the identical
methodology detailed in Appendix C.3 and Appendix C.4.

B. SafeCompass Algorithm
Our method operates exclusively during the chain-of-thought generation phase, ensuring that the final response generation
remains unaffected. As outlined in Algorithm 1, the process begins by initializing the context C with the input query x.
Throughout the thinking phase, we implement a periodic monitoring strategy at intervals of n tokens. At each checkpoint
(i.e., when t mod n = 0), the safety score At is computed according to Eq. (2). If this score falls below the predefined
threshold α, indicating a potential safety deviation, the safety guidance prompt Sguide is immediately injected into the
context. The model then continues its autoregressive generation, incorporating this guidance to steer the reasoning trajectory
back towards safety.

Algorithm 1 SafeCompass Inference
  Input: Input Query x, Threshold α, Interval n, Guidance Sguide
  Initialize context C ← x, step t ← 0
  while thinking phase continues do
     if t mod n = 0 then
        Calculate safety score At via Eq. (2)
        if At < α then
           C ← C ⊕ Sguide
        end if
     end if
     xnext ∼ M(C); C ← C ⊕ xnext ; t ← t + 1
  end while

General Benchmarks. For GSM8K and MMLU-Pro, which are composed of choice questions, we evaluated performance

C.5. Details of SafeCompass
In our main experiments and supplementary evaluations, unless otherwise specified, we universally configure the intervention
threshold α to −0.2 and the monitoring interval n to 200 tokens across all models. Regarding computational mechanics, the
calculation of safety score in SafeCompass necessitates a forward pass on two parallel branches where the KV cache cannot
be reused. A similar condition applies when injecting safety guidance tokens. However, as demonstrated in Section 5, the
overhead incurred by these limited additional forward passes is minimal, resulting in a maximum runtime increase of only
10%.

for Obfuscation. Obfuscation-based attacks necessitate a significantly higher frequency of interventions compared to
prefix/suffix-based and injection attacks, which typically require only a single intervention. This disparity arises because

during decoding, as shown in Figure 15. Consequently, single-step defenses are rendered insufficient, necessitating repeated
interventions to counteract this “decoding-time degradation.” 2) Impact of Model Scale. We observe a decrease in
intervention frequency as model size increases. Larger models generally possess superior instruction-following capabilities

D.2. Ablation Study
Figure 12 and Table 4 delineate the distribution of safety scores and performance metrics across various dimensions under
different ablation settings. Our detailed analysis is as follows:
i. Removing Control Branch: In this setting, we directly utilize the safety hidden state hsafety as the safety direction,
omitting the subtraction of hcontrol . This approach results in excessive semantic overlap between the derived concept and the
current state ht . Consequently, this overlap artificially inflates the safety score calculation, preventing the detection of actual
risks and ultimately causing a complete failure in defense effectiveness.
ii. Removing Token Sequence: Here, we remove the padding tokens from the control sequence, setting Scontrol = St . This
introduces significant positional discrepancies—alongside semantic differences—between hsafety and hcontrol . As a result,
the true safety direction dsafety cannot be accurately isolated. This distortion manifests as consistently erroneous low safety
scores, which trigger aggressive, misguided interventions and significantly escalate the over-refusal rate.
iii. Modify Punctuation: We observed that modifying the safety-related sentence to end with a period instead of a comma

F. Limitations and Future Work
Although SafeCompass demonstrates a superior trade-off between defense efficacy and utility preservation, there remains
room for further optimization. While the current implementation relies on top-layer states and static hyperparameters,
future work will explore identifying specific internal layers most correlated with safety to enhance detection sensitivity.
Furthermore, we aim to develop adaptive mechanisms that dynamically learn the optimal intervention threshold (α) and
monitoring step size (n) based on real-time inference contexts. Concurrently, we plan to investigate direct manipulation of
internal states during the CoT process—rather than explicit sequence insertion—to more directly and effectively modulate
internal safety levels, extending this exploration to broader domains.
